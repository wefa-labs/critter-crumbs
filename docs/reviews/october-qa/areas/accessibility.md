# Accessibility

One of the areas of the [October QA pass](../README.md). 15 findings; of 27 things checked, 18 passed, 8 failed, 1 were not run.

I covered all ten items of the brief on the accessibility stack: 260 audited captures (every fixture state and the live flow at 375x812 and 1440x900, plus 320 px, 200% zoom, doubled text, short phones and landscape), a whole feast from connect to results with no pointer in Chromium and again in WebKit, the focus ring measured from pixels on 941 tab stops, every row of the announcement table watched with times, and the motion, contrast and sound preferences. The feast can be completed by keyboard alone in both engines, every control has a name, every text pair passes contrast (lowest 4.87:1), targets meet their sizes, reduced motion and Still critters do what the spec says, and the two announcers are the only live regions. What fails is eight things I rate major: the focus ring on the critters cannot be seen over the painting; focus is dropped to the page in eleven places, including when scattering closes; a screen reader is never told the crumbs shown on the name tags or that scattering has closed; a visitor on a public result cannot turn the music off or still the critters; who gave what cannot be scrolled by keyboard; the game falls apart at 200% zoom on a phone; and on short phones the end of a long statement sits under the dock. Most of these are small changes (a CSS rule, a label, one announce call). Screen readers on real phones were not run; the script for people is in forPhones.

## Findings

### accessibility-01

**The focus ring on the critters is one thin dark line over a dark painting, the ring on + and − fades with the button when the pile is empty, and with a statement open focus goes to critters hidden under it.**

- **Severity:** major. **Call:** fix. **The finder's confidence:** confirmed. **Engines:** Chromium and WebKit, 375x812 and 1440x900.
- **Verifier:** reproduced; rates it major. Both builds. I measured it my own way, close to the finder's: the same clip photographed without focus and with keyboard focus, every pixel that changed taken as ring, and its contrast worked out against the same pixel without the ring (decoded in a blank silenced page, critters stilled so nothing else moves; on the production bundle two unfocused frames differed by 0 px). Chromium, 375x812, eight critters. Development build (version 2 portraits, no canvas): the critter buttons carry '2px solid rgb(46, 34, 38)', offset 2 px, no box-shadow, and the share of the ring that reaches 3:1 against the painting under it is 43, 50, 31, 30, 37, 20, 6 and 14 percent (medians 1.8 to 3.0 to 1). Production bundle (version 1, 3D drawn, 1 canvas, 8 meshes): the same rule, and 27, 24, 40, 20, 7, 12, 43 and 50 percent. For comparison on the same screens the Next arrow, with the design's 3 px ring, is 75 percent at a median 12.7:1. So the faint ring is on what members will get, not only on the development critters. Second part, development build: + with nothing left to give is aria-disabled, still a tab stop, drawn at opacity 0.4 with its ring: 0 percent of 702 ring pixels reach 3:1 (median 2.13), where the minus button beside it is 75 percent at 9.7:1. The second and third parts are the same CSS and markup in both builds (game.css:40, parts.tsx:177-178); I did not repeat them on the production bundle. ### accessibility-02

**Keyboard focus is dropped to the page in eleven places, among them the moment scattering closes and the feast begins.**

- **Severity:** major. **Call:** fix. **The finder's confidence:** confirmed. **Engines:** Chromium and WebKit, 375x812.
- **Verifier:** reproduced; rates it minor. Does not depend on the build (no drawing is involved; the controls are the same code). I reran four of the eleven places myself on verify-b in Chromium, each with the control focused and pressed by keyboard, and all four ended with document.activeElement on BODY: 1. Re-roll in the first-run dialog ('Re-roll (4 left)' before, BODY after); 2. Save statement after a save that succeeded (the 'Saved.' chip appears, focus on BODY); 4. Enter on a name tag (the circle turns to that critter, focus on BODY); 5. the deadline passing with focus on + (state 'closed', the three controls disabled, focus on BODY). What still works, also seen: Enter on Next and on Previous keeps focus on the button, and when the feast ended with nobody having scattered focus went to the Results heading, as the design asks. Not rerun, so I do not vouch for them: 3 (an arrow key on + or -; my check landed on the member's own critter, which has no + to focus), 6 (the waiting sheet replacing the dock; my feast had no scatter, so it went straight to results), and 7 to 11. The cause is the same in each, read in the source: a button that disables or unmounts itself while it holds focus (gathering.tsx:23 and app.tsx:102 'disabled={busy}', scatter.tsx:147 key={target.id}, parts.tsx:187). Why I rate it below the finder: the spec line it cites, Design System 12.5 (docs/02-design-system.md:886), lists five moments where focus must be placed, and the finder's own report says all five work and that a whole feast was finished by keyboard in both engines with nothing unreachable, because the next Tab carries on from near where the control was. So no specified state is wrong, and whether WCAG 2.4.3 is failed is arguable when the order of tab stops is kept. What is really lost is the arrow keys (their handler is on the game, which no longer holds focus) and, possibly, a screen reader's place, and nobody has run a screen reader on a real phone in this pass. If that run shows the reading position jumping to the top of the page at the close of scattering, I would move this back to major. Not decided or accepted anywhere I could find. Evidence: .scratch/qa/verify-b/d-gathering.mjs, out/d-gathering-chromium.log (places 1 and 2); .scratch/qa/verify-b/d-scatter.mjs, out/d-scatter-chromium.log (places 4 and 5)
- **Where:** Controls that disable or remove themselves while they hold focus: packages/app/src/app/app.tsx:102 (Re-roll), gathering.tsx:23 (Save statement), scatter.tsx:147 with game/parts.tsx:168 (an arrow key on − or +), parts.tsx:187 (a name tag), scatter.tsx:46 and :54 (−, + and the field disabled at closing), the dock replaced by the waiting sheet (results.tsx:66), sheets.tsx:63 (the confirmation's button), result-record.tsx:42, results.tsx:79 and surface.tsx:95 (Record, Replay Feast, the results' close icon). ui.tsx:39 can only return focus to an opener that is still there and enabled.
- **Expected:** docs/02-design-system.md §12.5: “Focus management: route change → #content; statement reveal → Start reading; scatter saved → the done line (tabindex="-1"); dialog close → opener; feast settled (or skipped, or reduced motion) → results <h1>.” and “Nothing steals focus while typing or pouring.” docs/03-game-design.md §4.2: “Skip to results is present from the first frame.” WCAG 2.4.3 Focus Order.
- **Observed:** After each of these document.activeElement is BODY, in both engines unless said: 1 Re-roll (“focus dropped; it was on button.secondary “Re-roll (4 left)””; four re-rolls means finding the button four times). 2 Save statement (“XX focus stays on Save statement (focus is on BODY)”). 3 An arrow key with focus on − or + (lifecycle-06: “ArrowRight with focus on +: Moss → Fern; focus on BODY”). 4 Pressing a name tag (“Enter on the name tag … “Clover”: … focus on BODY”). 5 The deadline passing with focus on −, the field or + (“focus dropped; it was on … “Give a crumb to Clover””). 6 The waiting sheet replacing the dock, whatever had focus (“= the sheet replaced the dock: focus on BODY”, four runs), so the feast begins with focus on the page and Skip to results is one Tab past every critter: 9 presses with eight critters. 7 A save that fails after being confirmed by keyboard (“state failed; focus BODY”, seen once; Scatter These is then 13 to 23 Tabs away). 8 Connecting again when Scatter These asks for it (seen once, WebKit). 9 Record the rehearsal result. 10 Replay Feast. 11 The close icon on the results. In both engines the next Tab carries on from near where the control was, so nothing becomes unreachable; but the arrow keys stop working (their handler is on the game, which no longer holds focus) and a screen reader's place is lost. What works: every route change puts focus on main#content; dialogs take focus and Escape returns it to the opener (Connect, Scatter These, the profile button); Start reading, the done line and the Results heading take focus when they should; a pour and typing keep focus through a re-read.
