# Sound

One of the areas of the [October QA pass](../README.md). 13 findings; of 15 things checked, 10 passed, 5 failed.

All thirteen items were run on the sound stack (https://127.0.0.1:5204) in Chromium, and all but the first in WebKit as well, in whole or in part, with every page silenced and the app's own graph metered on the silencer's bus once per render quantum; nothing reached the speakers (the meter's reading after the silencer was exactly zero in every run). The engine is sound where it was built to be: one AudioContext, nothing made or fetched before a gesture, no unhandled rejection for any kind of failed file, music that never stacks, sources and timers that do not grow, loop joins that match the decoded buffer to within 0.01 dB over three to five passes of all four tracks in both engines, the feast's fanfare within 0.1 s of the settle for 3, 8 and 10 critters, Skip silent in about 110 ms, and no sample at full scale even with far more cued at once than a screen allows (worst render -0.7 dB). Three things are worth fixing before the feast, all small: coming back to a hidden tab plays about 9 ms of whatever was sounding when it was hidden (the limiter's look-ahead; up to -5 dB in the feast); on a slow page the pour's grains bunch into loud bursts, the machine gun the spec rules out; and scattering closing under the open screen is never put in the polite announcer, so for a screen-reader member the Dusk sting carries that meaning alone. The rest are measurements and calls for Afolabi: one failed request silences a loop for as long as the screen keeps its track and costs the member the feast cue on their one live feast; superseded loads cost 56 s of waiting on a 400 kbit/s link; quick movement between screens leaves up to 350 MB of decoded tracks waiting for collection; a setting changed in one tab does not reach another; two loops drop about 8 dB at their join; the last crumb plays the boing with the ta-da; and a feast opened from a link plays in silence because no gesture has been made yet. Whether any of it is audible, and how the pour behaves on the council's phones, needs real devices: those checks are listed under forPhones.

## Findings

### sound-01

**Coming back to a hidden tab plays about 9 ms of whatever was sounding when it was hidden.**

- **Severity:** minor. **Call:** fix. **The finder's confidence:** confirmed. **Engines:** Chromium and WebKit.
- **Where:** Any screen with sound playing when the tab is hidden. packages/app/src/audio/index.ts:58 (setHidden: silence, then suspend) and packages/app/src/audio/voices.ts:19-23 (the limiter, a DynamicsCompressorNode, ends the chain; the voices' compressor sits before it).
- **Expected:** docs/03-game-design.md §5.7: "when the tab is hidden everything ends at once, because a suspended context would keep a fade and play it on return." §5.5: "While the tab is hidden all sound stops; the music returns over 1 s."
- **Observed:** Sources and voices are stopped and the context suspended, but the two compressors keep the last 6 ms they were given in their look-ahead, and the suspended context plays it when it resumes. With a wall-clock stamp on every render quantum, the first quanta after a hide of 3.4 to 4.6 s read (dB peak on the bus; one quantum is 2.9 ms): Chromium, hidden 120 ms after the fanfare: -8.3, -8.9, -11.6, then -36; hidden in the feast's wave: -22.7, -20.3, -30.3, then exact silence until the next feed; a held pour: -18.2, -17.4, -19.9, -20.6, -24.3 (five quanta: two look-aheads), then -59; the reveal's sting: -24.1, -21.7, -32.6, then -97; music alone inside a loop's seam: -23.0, -23.5, -26.3, then -61 and rising (the one-second return). WebKit: feast wave -12.8, -5.2, -9.0, then silence; after the fanfare -12.9, -13.8, -15.5; sting -25.3, -26.1, -33.8; seam -26.4, -24.0, -23.6. So every return to the app while music plays begins with a 9 ms fragment at the music's full level before the music fades up from nothing, and a return during the feast with one at up to -5 dB. Nothing else is replayed.
- **To reproduce:** mise exec node@22.22.1 -- node .scratch/qa/sound/05-hidden.mjs chromium a b c d   (then: webkit). Each row prints aroundTheSuspension: wallGapMs is the time hidden, firstQuantaAfterDb the bus after the return.

### sound-02

**On a slow page a held pour sounds as loud bursts of bells, not a pour.**

- **Severity:** minor. **Call:** fix. **The finder's confidence:** confirmed. **Engines:** Chromium with the main thread stalled; WebKit on the dev stack as served (frames of 75 to 80 ms there, known as lifecycle-15).
- **Where:** The scatter screen, holding + or -. packages/app/src/audio/engine.ts:66-72 (pump starts every grain that is due at Math.max(now, at)), packages/app/src/audio/plan.ts:83 (grains() hands back up to 16 overdue grains at once), packages/app/src/audio/index.ts:36 (setInterval(pump, 25) on the main thread).
- **Expected:** docs/03-game-design.md §5.3: "A hold must sound like an unbroken pour, never a machine gun." §5.7: "The pour scheduler runs on a 25 ms timer and the music's loop scheduler on a 1 s timer, never on requestAnimationFrame, so a dropped frame never stutters the sound."
- **Observed:** The 25 ms timer is still on the page's main thread, so a long frame delays it, and when it runs late every grain that fell due is started at the same instant. Three-second hold, Music off so the bus carries the pour alone. Chromium, main thread blocked for N ms of every N+50: none: timer every 27 ms (median), never two bells at one instant, bus peak -16.0 dB; 60 ms: two at once, -15.4 dB; 120 ms: up to four at once, -11.1 dB; 200 ms: timer every 207 ms, up to seven bells at one instant, -8.4 dB. WebKit on the dev stack with nothing added, two runs: the timer ran 14 and 15 times in 3 s (median gap 165 and 163 ms), up to five bells at one instant with four or more at 4 and 5 instants, bus peak -9.3 and -9.2 dB. With music playing and the machine busier the same hold in WebKit peaked at -3.7 dB against -14.5 dB in Chromium (09-reduced). So where frames are slow the pour is a cluster of bells every 160 to 250 ms, 7 to 12 dB louder than it should be. Production frame times on the council's phones are not known to me (production-build-13 measured 60 frames a second in WebKit on this machine), so how often this is heard is a real-phone check; the mechanism is not in doubt.
- **To reproduce:** mise exec node@22.22.1 -- node .scratch/qa/sound/15-pour-jank.mjs chromium   and   mise exec node@22.22.1 -- node .scratch/qa/sound/15-pour-jank.mjs webkit stall=0,0   (prints the timer's gaps, the most bells started at one instant, and the bus peak)
- **Evidence:** .scratch/qa/sound/out/15-pour-jank-chromium.log, .scratch/qa/sound/out/15-pour-jank-webkit.log, .scratch/qa/sound/out/15-pour-envelope-*.json, .scratch/qa/sound/out/09-reduced-webkit.log (ordinary hold busPeakDb -3.66), .scratch/qa/sound/out/09-reduced-chromium.json (-14.46)

### sound-04

### sound-06

**Moving quickly between screens leaves up to 350 MB of decoded tracks waiting for the garbage collector.**

- **Severity:** minor. **Call:** decide. **The finder's confidence:** confirmed. **Engines:** Chromium (memory by forced collection and process size) and WebKit (by what is still reachable; collection cannot be forced there).
- **Where:** Any change between screens with different tracks, and the Music toggle. packages/app/src/audio/music.ts:25 (release on leaving) and :36 (a load that arrives late is decoded, then released).
- **Expected:** docs/03-game-design.md §5.5: "One track at a time. A track is fetched and decoded when a screen first wants it, and let go when another takes over: a decoded track is about 30 MB."
- **Observed:** The app does let go: after a forced collection 39.6 MB is alive (the playing track and the recordings) and AudioHandlers is 17, as at the start. But each visit that lasts long enough for its load to land decodes 20 to 32 MB afresh, and nothing frees it until the collector runs. Fifty changes of screen in about 70 s, Chromium: 45 decodes, 1,028 MB decoded in all (The burrow 12 times, The town square 10, Feeding time 11), up to 349.9 MB of decoded tracks alive at once between collections, and the page's process between 358 and 1,010 MB in the samples taken every fifth step against 150 MB before. WebKit: 43 decodes, 966 MB, up to 194.8 MB alive at once, four tracks decoded and never played. Twenty-one clicks on the Music toggle in a second: 4 or 5 full requests and decodes of the track, 103 MB (Chromium) and 255 MB (WebKit) alive at the end of that test. /audio/ has no caching rule (production-build-14), so in production each of those is also a request to revalidate. On a desktop this corrects itself; whether a mid-range phone reloads the tab under it, losing a scatter not yet saved, is a real-phone check.
- **To reproduce:** mise exec node@22.22.1 -- node .scratch/qa/sound/07-churn.mjs chromium   (then: webkit); the line "end" has decodesInAll, maxAliveDecodedMBSeenBetweenCollections and the figures before and after a collection

## Not run

- Real throttling of the whole page on the production bundle: whether the 1.85 MB of sound asked for at the first gesture slows Connect or the lazy chunks. Only the sound stack was mine (a dev server over HTTP/1.1 behind a proxy), so the slow link was modelled inside the test for /audio/ requests only.
- The production bundle altogether (3D critters, the page policy): not my stack. In particular how often a production page stalls long enough to bunch the pour (sound-02).
- Which event unlocks sound in a real browser: automation starts audio with no gesture (already known). Real tab hiding: a headless browser never hides a page, so document.hidden, visibilitychange, window blur and the stopping of animation frames were emulated; timer throttling in a hidden tab was not.
- Garbage collection cannot be forced in WebKit, so its "alive" figures are what had not yet been collected, not what was retained; process memory was read for Chromium only.
- Slow network (item 1) in WebKit. Toggles in mid-sting, mid-pour, mid-feast and at a loop's seam (item 3) in WebKit. The feasts for 8 and 10 critters, and the live worst case (item 9), in WebKit. Each ran in Chromium.
- A desktop viewport: every run was at 390 by 844. That the music does not step back under a statement on desktop was read in the code (scatter.tsx:115) and not measured.
- The feast where nobody scattered and the reveal replaying on every visit: already known and undecided, not looked at again.
- Loudness and balance by ear, and whether sound-01, -02, -09 and -10 are audible: nobody may hear this pass.
