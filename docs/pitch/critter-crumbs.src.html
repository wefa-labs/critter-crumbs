Everyone gets a handful of crumbs to share.

A private way for a group to decide how money gets shared out. Everyone is given the same handful. Where yours go stays yours until the feast, and then it all opens at once.

Four people, one pot of money,
and no good way to split it.

  • Someone says a number first, and it becomes the number everyone edges away from.
  • The loud work gets counted. The quiet work that held it together does not.
  • When everyone can see who gave what, giving turns into trading favours.
  • Or it goes to a show of hands, and the best known person wins.

Critter Crumbs changes one thing.
Nobody sees who gave what until it is all in.

  • There is no first number to react to. Everyone gives at the same time, in private.
  • Everyone writes what they did, and it all opens at once, so quiet work gets read.
  • Nobody can see what you gave, so there is nothing to trade back.
  • Nobody can read the room, because there is no room to read.

Built first for one council of 6–10 people. It fits any group that shares out money or credit: a co-op, a grant programme, a project team.

A feast, start to finish.

Six steps, two deadlines, and one moment when every scatter opens at once.

  1. Start a burrow Make the group once and add the people. It is kept, and used again for the next feast.
  2. Open a feast Name what is being shared out, set two deadlines, and give everyone the same handful of crumbs.
  3. Gathering closes Gathering Everyone writes down what they did. Nobody can read anyone else’s yet.
  4. Scattering closes Scattering The statements open. Everyone reads them, then scatters their crumbs in private.
  5. The feast Every scatter opens at once, on every device, to the same numbers. Nobody has to press anything.
  6. The result Each critter’s share of the pot, ready to act on. Who else sees it is the burrow’s choice.

Who can see what, at each step

  • Everyone in the burrow sees who is in it.
  • The burrow sees what is being shared out, and by when.
  • Only you can see your statement.
  • Everyone sees every statement. Nobody sees any scatter, not even us.
  • Everyone sees the same numbers, at the same moment.
  • The burrow sees the totals, or anyone with the link. Who gave what: as the burrow chose.

Three phases, two deadlines.

Gathering closes in 2 days Scattering closes in 5 days
  • GatheringEveryone writes down what they did. Nobody can read anyone else’s yet, and you can keep editing yours.
  • ScatteringEvery statement opens at the same moment. You read them, then you give. You can change your mind right up to the deadline.
  • The feastEverything opens. The critters wake and grow, and the numbers are there.

Both deadlines stay on screen, in your own time zone. The clock is the server’s, not your phone’s. Nobody starts the feast. It arrives.

Five rules, and the app holds everyone to them.

  • Equal crumbsEveryone in a feast gets the same number. The creator picks it once, and 100 is the usual answer.
  • Give all of themYou cannot submit with crumbs left in your hand.
  • Never to yourselfYour own critter is there, asleep, quietly eating what other people give it.
  • Sitting out costs nothingYou still receive. The burrow is simply told that you did not give.
  • Shares come from what was givenNot from what was handed out. Someone sitting out cannot quietly water everyone else down.

Then, once, the feast.

Every critter wakes together, before any of them is fed. The first thing the feast says is that we are all here.

Crumbs fly. Critters chew and grow. The light turns golden.

One small hop together, and the numbers are simply there.

No confetti. No leaderboard. No spotlight on whoever came first. A critter that got few crumbs is calm, not sad.

What you are promised.

  • No one can read what you gave until the feastYour crumbs are locked to a time, not to a person. A group of key holders hands over the key when the deadline passes. Not before, and there is no one to ask.
  • We cannot make one upYours is signed by you. One we invented would not carry your signature.
  • We cannot quietly lose oneThe full list goes up the moment scattering closes. Check that yours is on it.
  • Everyone gets the same numbersThe count runs on your own device. There is no server answer for anyone to argue with.
  • Nothing about you is measuredNo analytics and no telemetry. The app never records what you looked at, how long you took, or what you clicked. It makes the network requests it needs to do its job, and nothing else.

The two switches are independent, so every pairing is available. Two things never change: whether you took part is visible to your burrow from the start, and where your crumbs went is hidden from everyone until the feast.

We hold your scatter. We cannot open it.

One thing we will not pretend

Before the feast, nobody can read your scatter, because nobody has the key. Not us, not the creator. After the feast the key exists, so keeping who gave what private becomes a rule we enforce rather than something the lock does for us.

Three chains underneath, and nobody has to notice.

Scatters are signed and held by us, never written to a chain. Only a public result is written down, and that is the one time anyone pays a fee. The key is split across Shutter’s holders, so none of them can open a feast alone.

October works if all four of these hold.

  • Nobody needs helpThe feast runs start to finish without anyone asking how it works.
  • Every scatter arrivesEveryone finds their own on the published list, and every one of them opens.
  • Everyone sees the same numbersAny two devices land on the same result, and the public record agrees.
  • The money can moveThe result goes into the budget document without anyone retyping it.

Where it goes next.

  • Sign in with an email Nobody ever sees a seed phrase, and nothing on screen mentions a chain. The account layer underneath is already shaped for this, so it is one layer changing rather than a rewrite.
  • Nobody pays to take part Fees are sponsored, so the one remaining cost disappears and taking part is free from end to end.
  • The feast holds the money A feast takes custody of the funds and lets them go according to its own result, the moment it opens. No operator, no payment run, nobody in the middle deciding.
  • Help writing your statement When a feast opens you get a prompt to take to whatever assistant you already use, along with your own notes. It knows the feast, the deadlines and the length limit, and it helps you pull out what you actually did. Your notes never come near us.

Custody is the part worth funding. Before it, this is a tool for deciding. With it, the decision carries itself out.
