Everyone gets a handful of crumbs to share.

A private way for a group to decide how something gets shared out. Everyone is given the same handful. Where yours go stays yours until the feast, and then it all opens at once.

Four people, one pot of money,
and no good way to split it.

  • Someone says a number first, and it becomes the number everyone edges away from.
  • The loud work gets counted. The quiet work that held it together does not.
  • When everyone can see who gave what, giving turns into trading favours.
  • Or it goes to a show of hands, and the best known person wins.

Critter Crumbs changes one thing. Nobody sees where anyone’s crumbs went until every crumb is in.

Everything else follows from that. The rules, the deadlines, the encryption, even the small creatures. All of it exists to protect the moment when it opens.

Everyone gets the same handful.

100 crumbs each. No weighting, no seniority, no one holding more than anyone else.

You have to give all of them away. You cannot keep any back, and you cannot feed yourself.

Three phases, two deadlines.

Gathering closes in 2 days Scattering closes in 5 days
  • GatheringEveryone writes down what they did. Nobody can read anyone else’s yet, and you can keep editing yours.
  • ScatteringEvery statement opens at the same moment. You read them, then you give. You can change your mind right up to the deadline.
  • The feastEverything opens. The critters wake and grow, and the numbers are there.

Both deadlines are on screen the whole time, in your own time zone. The clock is the server’s, never your phone’s. And nobody presses a button to start the feast. It simply arrives.

Five rules, and the count enforces every one.

  • Equal crumbsEveryone in a feast gets the same number. The creator picks it once, and 100 is the usual answer.
  • Give all of themYou cannot submit with crumbs left in your hand.
  • Never to yourselfYour own critter is there, asleep, quietly eating what other people give it.
  • Sitting out costs nothingYou still receive. The burrow is simply told that you did not give.
  • Shares come from what was givenNot from what was handed out. Someone sitting out cannot quietly water everyone else down.

What you are promised.

  • No one can read what you gave until the feastYour crumbs are locked to a time, not to a person. A group of key holders hands over the key when the deadline passes. Not before, and there is no one to ask.
  • We cannot make one upYours is signed by you. One we invented would not carry your signature.
  • We cannot quietly lose oneThe full list goes up the moment scattering closes. Check that yours is on it.
  • Everyone gets the same numbersThe count runs on your own device. There is no server answer for anyone to argue with.
  • Nothing about you is measuredNo analytics. No telemetry. A tool built on privacy should not phone home.

How it works underneath.

One thing we will not pretend

Once the key is out, anyone holding the locked scatters can open them. Keeping who gave what hidden after the feast is a promise about what we show and who we hand data to. It is not maths. Before the feast it is maths, and that is the part that matters most.

Then, once, the feast.

Everyone wakes together, before anyone is fed. The first thing it says is that we are all here.

Crumbs fly. Critters chew and grow. The light turns golden.

One small hop together, and the numbers are simply there.

No confetti. No leaderboard. No spotlight on whoever came first. A critter that got few crumbs is calm, not sad.
