## Recommended sequence

### 1. Establish the visual target and implementation baseline

Use the current deployed baseline, not this older documentation branch, for implementation. Record reference states and exact address/variant/version inputs in the existing review/gallery workflow. Keep existing worktrees and unrelated changes intact.

Claude Code produces a compact reference set: launcher, tutorial results, combined first meeting, home/burrow card, avatar menu, connection states, and feast wizard. Show phone and desktop, light and dark. Existing Inked daylight decisions remain the starting point: warm palette, restrained ink outlines, gold primary action, coherent type scale, and painted world. The new dark look needs the same care.

Explore three creature directions side by side:

1. **Faithful v2:** establish how much of the mismatch is version selection and rendering fidelity.
2. **Garden spirits:** more varied earth/water/fire/air silhouettes, grounded and flying bodies, nature expressed through anatomy, drawing from the existing creature research.
3. **Painted companions:** a smaller, carefully authored family of bodies with deterministic feature variation, assessed against the project's painted creature references.

Compare faces at 40/64 px and full bodies at their real scene sizes. Review silhouette, eye expression, anatomy, foot contact/shadow, lighting, texture, and animation together. Do not approve an isolated portrait and assume it works in every scene. Do not silently redraw existing locked identities or freeze an onchain recipe while the visual direction is unsettled.

### 2. Codex builds the common shell and stable surfaces

Implement the agreed viewport shell, bounded content regions, common modal/sheet structure, avatar menu, and application feedback. Include loading, empty, error, reconnection, and long-content states at the start.

Repair navigation and small interaction gaps within those surfaces: left-aligned creation, whole burrow cards, reachable multiple-burrow creation, copy link/address confirmation, shorter removal copy, discoverable appearance settings, and ENS feedback. Reuse existing settings, announcements, copy IDs, resolver, and native dialog behavior.

Claude owns reference dimensions and visual tokens; Codex owns responsive CSS, semantics, focus, and state behavior. After the structural pass, Claude reviews and adjusts visual treatment in a scoped pass. Assign one writer to shared CSS/tokens at a time.

### 3. Complete first meeting and learning

Use one first-meeting surface with a prominent critter preview and the name alongside/below it. Reconnects and different invite routes must not lose their destination or bypass API membership rules. Verified ENS users still meet their critter without being forced to enter a redundant name. A half-completed first meeting must resume coherently. Apply the already approved 16-character typed-name rule consistently across the form, API, and option editor; preserve full ENS names while shortening their visual display.

Extend How it works from practice into a **simulated feast and results**, explicitly labelled as an example. Show how complete private scatters become totals and proportions, how someone who did not scatter can still receive, and where the group can see participation. Use four critters with 100 crumbs each, three of whom scatter. Totals of 120/90/60/30 produce 40%/30%/20%/10%; the fourth critter receives 30 without scattering. The denominator is the 300 crumbs actually scattered. One valid example is A giving B/C/D 60/30/10, B giving A/C/D 60/30/10, and C giving A/B/D 60/30/10. No one gives to themselves.

Keep the ordinary explanation short, replayable, and available without connecting. The visitor should be able to answer what they give, what stays hidden, when it opens, and what a result means. Do not make architecture study a condition of participating.

### 4. Replace feast setup with four bounded steps

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Title, context, and people/options choice. |
| Critters/options | People roster and minimum/capacity explanation, or a compact option list with one name/statement editor at a time. |
| Timing and visibility | Gathering/scattering deadlines, time zone, visibility choices; 100 crumbs remains the ordinary default, with deliberate access to changing it. |
| Review | Plain-language summary, edit links, and save draft. Opening the feast remains the existing distinct action. |

Desktop uses one stable modal frame; mobile uses one stable bottom sheet within the available viewport. Header and Back/Next/Save actions stay put. Long option lists and statements scroll inside the body; adding an option must not grow the entire modal or page. Back and validation preserve all entered values. Cancellation with unsaved work has an explicit recovery decision. Keep the existing server-time validation, privacy combinations, target ordering, and participant-freeze behavior.

### 5. Claude finishes art, motion, and sound against working screens

Apply the selected creature direction, scene scale, lighting, and tokens to the real journey. Match the approved reference at the same viewport, version, and seed, including reduced-motion/simple-view equivalents. Leave text and interactive layout in the DOM.

For music, Claude checks each loop across several repetitions and prepares an intentional musical boundary rather than using a blanket overlap to hide it. Codex implements per-track playback metadata and tests timing, cleanup, visibility changes, ducking, and transitions. Audit all music tracks, not just the launcher. Do not simply set the existing fade to zero without checking the source audio for silence, tails, or clicks.

For feedback, use a small response with a clear outcome: a crumb hop and “Link copied,” for example. Keep success brief and nonblocking, failures readable and recoverable, and every sound paired with visible state. The reveal and results huddle should feel coordinated and welcoming without making low totals or absence feel punitive.

### 6. Optional architecture and identity work, with explicit boundaries

“How it's built” should show a short, layered explanation:

1. A connected account proves control of an address and signs a scatter.
2. The API/database stores burrows, profiles, plaintext statements, and encrypted scatters. It hides statements until scattering; Shutter does not encrypt those statements. The server knows membership and metadata.
3. EAS structures the signed **offchain** scatter. Signing is not the same as posting a transaction.
4. Shutter registers the feast's encryption identity and closing time, and its keypers release the decryption key. Each client decrypts and tallies locally.
5. Base is the intended destination for an optional public result attestation. The inspected UI only supports a development rehearsal recorder; label that limitation and testnet status accurately. Recording results does not distribute money.

Normal screens retain the project's friendly vocabulary. Technical names belong in this optional explanation and necessary signing/cost details. Changes to copy guidance should document this intentional scope.

The [identity research](../research/critter-identity-options-2026-10-15.md) recommends preserving versioned generator/renderer artifacts and independent fixtures first. A project-level Base/EAS record of a cryptographic manifest hash could later establish recipe provenance without exposing burrow membership. An address-bound personal choice, its privacy, recovery, permanence, and gas payer are separate decisions. A hash is not artifact hosting, and an offchain renderer referenced by a hash is not onchain execution. No NFT or public membership registry is required.

Treat the production result-recorder gap as a separately scoped functional issue. It should not be hidden by revised wording, folded into creature registration, or represented as already working.

## Quality gates

- **Visual:** side-by-side references and actual screenshots for the connected journey, in both themes, on phone and desktop. Same data, addresses, variants, and generation version. Include long names, eight/ten critters, long statements, many options, and empty/error/loading states. Judge art and composition, not only bounding boxes.
- **Layout:** no unintended document overflow on normal app screens at 375 × 812, 390 × 844, 1280 × 720, and 1440 × 900; add a short phone viewport and real mobile Safari/browser-keyboard checks. Internal content remains reachable with browser zoom and large text. Do not obtain “no scrolling” by clipping essential content.
- **Stable dialogs:** measure outer bounds through discovery → one/many options → waiting → retry/error. At the same viewport, the outer frame and action area must not jump. Test dynamic browser bars, safe areas, and keyboard resizing separately.
- **Behavior:** fresh invited/unknown/returning accounts; partial onboarding and reroll limits; ENS success/unresolved/outage; copy success/failure; keyboard whole-card navigation; multiple burrows; setup Back/edit/cancel/retry, maximum options, deadline failures, and draft/open distinction.
- **Accessibility and feel:** visible focus, focus return, understandable labels, 44 px controls, one announcement per action, adequate contrast, reduced motion, muted sound, and Simple view. Copy remains centralized. Notifications do not steal focus.
- **Core invariants:** retain equal complete scatters, no self-scatter in people feasts, participant freeze, latest accepted replacement, server-derived phase, client-side deterministic tally, encrypted allocations, and no analytics/telemetry.
- **Engineering:** focused behavioral regression tests through existing seams, appropriate existing browser checks, `bun run check`, and `bun run build` with the pinned toolchain. Use production builds as well as development previews to prevent another v1/v2 mismatch. Before merging, run the repository's standards/spec review. Listen to the shipped audio assets over repeated seams; arithmetic tests cannot approve music.
